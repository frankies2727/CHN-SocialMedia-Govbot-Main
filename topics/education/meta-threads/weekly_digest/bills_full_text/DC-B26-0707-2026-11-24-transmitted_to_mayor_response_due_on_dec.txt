AN ACT
_________________________
IN THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
_________________________
To amend subtitle B of the Rodent Control Act of 2000 to modify prohibited actions under the
act, to create a rodent abatement dashboard, and to require the Department of Health to
conduct a public education campaign on rodent prevention and control and rodenticide
health risks; to amend subtitle A of Title I of the Sustainable Solid Waste Management
Amendment Act to provide notice requirements before the removal of a public litter
container and to create a pilot program to provide grants to Business Improvement
Districts and Main Streets to subsidize or purchase rodent proof trash receptacles; to
amend the Pesticide Education and Control Amendment Act of 2012 to prohibit the sale
of anticoagulant rodenticide, and to limit the distribution and use of anticoagulant
rodenticides by persons not trained or licensed as pesticide applicators; and for other
purposes.
BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this
act may be cited as the “Rodent Education and Management Yields Results Amendment Act of
2026”.
Sec. 2. Subtitle B of the Rodent Control Act of 2000, effective October 19, 2000 (D.C.
Law 13-172; D.C. Official Code § 8-2103.01 et seq.), is amended as follows:
(a) Section 904 (D.C. Official Code § 8-2103.01) is amended by adding a new paragraph
(1A) to read as follows:
“(1A) “Bureau” means the Bureau of Rodent Control within the Department of
Health.”.
(b) Section 907 (D.C. Official Code § 8-2103.04) is amended as follows:
(1) The lead-in language of subsection (a) is amended by striking the phrase
“certain health hazards that have resulted from the harborage of rodents” and inserting the phrase
“violations of this subtitle” in its place.
(2) Subsection (b) is amended by striking the phrase “reasonable costs for
correcting” and inserting the phrase “reasonable costs, to total no more than 3 times the costs
incurred by the District, for correcting” in its place.

(c) Section 908 (D.C. Official Code § 8-2103.05) is amended as follows:
(1) The section heading is amended to read as follows:
“Sec. 908. Conditions contributing to rodent activity prohibited.”.
(2) Subsection (a) is amended to read as follows:
“(a) It shall be unlawful for any person to cause, permit, or maintain a condition on public
or private property that causes or enables ongoing rodent activity or infestation by providing
food, water, or harborage, including through the accumulation or improper storage of trash,
garbage, debris, discarded materials, abandoned or inoperable vehicles, standing water, or
permitting weeds or grass to grow to a height of 8 inches or more on private property which they
own.”.
(3) Subsection (c) is amended as follows:
(A) Strike the phrase “subsections (a) and (b)” and inserting the phrase
“subsection (a) or subsection (b)” in its place.
(B) Strike the phrase “the condition causing rodent harborage” both times
it appears and insert the phrase “the condition causing or contributing to rodent activity or
infestation” in its place.
(4) New subsections (e) and (f) are added to read as follows:
“(e) Nothing in this section shall be construed to prohibit the enforcement of either the
Litter Control Administrative Act of 1985, effective March 25, 1986 (D.C. Law 6-100; D.C.
Official Code § 8-801 et seq.), or the Vector-Borne Infectious Diseases Control Act of 2004,
effective May 18, 2004 (D.C. Law 15-163; D.C. Official Code § 8-2131.01 et seq.), if a
condition constitutes a violation of both subsection (a) of this section and one of those acts.
“(f) The Mayor may require a person who violates subsection (a) of this section to submit
and implement a rodent abatement plan to the Bureau in a form and manner prescribed by the
Mayor when the Mayor determines that a rodent abatement plan is necessary to eliminate rodent
activity or infestation.”.
(d) New sections 908a and 908b are added to read as follows:
“Sec. 908a. Rodent abatement dashboard.
“(a) By October 1, 2027, the Department of Health (“Department”) shall create a
dashboard on rodent abatement, which shall be prominently accessible on the Department’s
webpage. The dashboard shall include:
“(1) Time series geospatial data of treated rodent burrows, types of rodent
abatement treatments utilized, and rodent abatement treatment requests and resolution status; and
“(2) Filters to enable users to view data by Ward, Advisory Neighborhood
Commission, Single Member District, and calendar quarter.
“(b) The dashboard shall be machine-readable, enabled for source-data export,
maintained with time-series and geospatial data, and be capable of filtering data by attributes
through a graphical user interface.”.
“Sec. 908b. Public education on rodent abatement.

“(a) The Bureau shall establish a public education program (“program”) to reduce rodent
populations, which shall include information on:
“(1) Effective rodent prevention strategies, including sanitation, habitat
modification, and biological control;
“(2) Evidence-based rodent abatement strategies, including available government
and community resources;
“(3) Safe practices for encountering rodents to minimize risk of disease
transmission; and
“(4) Public health risks to children, adults, pets, and wildlife of improper use of
rodenticides, including emergency response procedures when a human or pet has ingested
rodenticide.
“(b) The program may:
“(1) Distribute printed materials, including brochures, mailers, or door hangers;
“(2) Create digital content, including website resources, social media, and
interactive tools;
“(3) Conduct in-person trainings and community events;
“(4) Take out bus and metro banners;
“(5) Conduct partnerships with community organizations, schools, faith-based
institutions, and business associations; and
“(6) Conduct direct outreach to high-priority parties, including food service
establishments, property managers and owners, landlords, event venues, and other parties near
areas with high rodent activity.”.
Sec. 3. Subtitle A of Title I of the Sustainable Solid Waste Management Amendment Act
of 2014, effective February 26, 2015 (D.C. Law 20-154; D.C. Official Code § 8-1031.01 et seq.),
is amended as follows:
(a) Section 106a (D.C. Official Code § 8-1031.06a) is amended as follows:
(1) The section heading is amended by striking the phrase “Installation of” and
inserting the phrase “Installation and removal of” in its place.
(2) A new subsection (c) is added to read as follows:
“(c) Before removing a public litter container on a block in a Residential Zone, DPW
shall:
“(1) Receive a request for removal;
“(2) Issue a written notice of intent to remove a public litter container to the
Council and the affected ANCs and provide the Council and affected ANCs 30 days, excluding
Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, to respond; and
“(3) Give great weight to any comment from an affected ANC, pursuant to section
13(d)(3)(A) of the Advisory Neighborhood Commissions Act of 1975, effective March 26, 1976
(D.C. Law 1-58; D.C. Official Code § 1-309.10(d)(3)(A)).”.

(b) A new section 106c is added to read as follows:
“Sec. 106c. Establishment of commercial corridor rodent-proof receptacle pilot grant
program.
“(a) The Department of Public Works shall establish a pilot grant program to provide
grants to install at least 10 rodent-proof receptacles for businesses in commercial corridors with
high rat populations.
“(b) To be eligible to participate in the pilot grant program, an applicant shall:
“(1) Be a Business Improvement District, Main Street, or private food-serving
business;
“(2) Be located in a commercial corridor where there is a high incidence of
rodents, according to the Department of Health’s rodent dashboard; and
“(3) Demonstrate that current receptacles are causing increased rodents in a
commercial corridor and have consent from a business to replace one or more current receptacles
with rodent-proof receptacles.
“(c) The Mayor shall monitor and evaluate the pilot program for a period of no less than
months following installation of the rodent-proof receptacles and assess program
effectiveness, including:
“(1) The reduction in rodent activity and complaints in pilot areas;
“(2) Business compliance with proper use of rodent-proof receptacles, including
adequate frequency of waste collection; and
“(3) Durability, maintenance requirements, and user satisfaction of different
receptacle designs, if grantees purchase different models.
“(d) Within 18 months of the pilot grant program’s commencement, the Department of
Public Works shall submit a report to the Council detailing the program’s findings,
recommendations for expanding the pilot program, and grantee feedback on the best-performing
rodent-proof receptacles.”.
(c) Section 114(c) (D.C. Official Code § 8-1031.14(c)) is amended as follows:
(1) The existing text is designated as paragraph (1).
(2) A new paragraph (2) is added to read as follows:
“(2) If during enforcement, there is evidence of active rodent or other vector
activity, the Department of Public Works shall refer the violation to the Department of Health as
set forth in sections 907 and 908 of the Rodent Control Act of 2000, effective February 2, 2001
(D.C. Law 13-172; D.C. Official Code §§ 8-2103.04 & -2103.05), and section 6 of the VectorBorne Infectious Diseases Control Act of 2004, effective May 18, 2004 (D.C. Law 15-163; D.C.
Official Code § 8-2131.05).”.
Sec. 4. The Pesticide Education and Control Amendment Act of 2012, effective October
23, 2012 (D.C. Law 19-191; D.C. Official Code § 8-431 et seq.), is amended as follows:
(a) Section 2 (D.C. Official Code § 8-431) is amended by adding a new paragraph (1A) to

read as follows:
“(1A) “Anticoagulant rodenticide” means a pesticide product intended to kill a rat
or mouse that has a mode of action that interferes with blood clotting, including products with
Brodifacoum, Bromadiolone, Chlorophacinone, Difenacoum, Difethialone, Diphacinonon, or
Warfarin, as active ingredients.”.
(b) Section 4 (D.C. Official Code § 8-433) is amended by adding a new subsection (d) to
read as follows:
“(d)(1) It shall be unlawful for a person or entity to sell or otherwise distribute for use
anticoagulant rodenticide, and for a person or entity to use anticoagulant rodenticide on public or
private property; except, that this subsection shall not apply to the distribution, purchase, or use
of anticoagulant rodenticide by a:
“(A) District or federal government agency acting within the scope of its
official duties;
“(B) Contractor acting on behalf of a local or federal government agency;
or
“(C) Company or person certified to apply pesticides by the Department of
Energy and the Environment, when acting within the scope of that certification.
“(2)(A) A person or entity who sells or distributes anticoagulant rodenticide in
violation of paragraph (1) of this subsection shall cease the activity within 24 hours of receiving
notice of the violation from the Mayor. A person or entity who fails to cease the activity after
receiving one notice of violation shall be subject to civil fines, penalties, and fees consistent with
section 10 of this act.
“(B) A person or entity who uses anticoagulant rodenticide in violation of
paragraph (1) of this subsection shall remove the anticoagulant rodenticide within 24 hours of
receiving notice of the violation from the Mayor. A person or entity who fails to remove the
rodenticide after receiving more than one notice of violation shall be subject to civil fines,
penalties, and fees consistent with section 10 of this act.
“(3) The Mayor may prohibit the use, sale, or distribution of additional types of
rodenticides based on documented evidence of harm to children or wildlife through rulemaking.
“(4) The Department may refer violations to the Attorney General, who may
institute proceedings in the Superior Court of the District of Columbia to enforce the provisions
of this section.”.

Sec. 6. Title II of the Trash Compactor Tax Incentive Amendment Act of 2014, effective
March 11, 2015 (D.C. Law 20-223; D.C. Official Code § 8-751 et seq.), is revived as of the
applicability date of this section, and amended to read as follows:
“TITLE II. TRASH COMPACTOR GRANT PROGRAM
“Sec. 201. Definitions.
“For the purposes of this title, the term:
“(1) “Business” means a single enterprise or a consortium of enterprises set up for
the common purpose of acquiring a commercial trash compactor for the use of each enterprise in
the consortium.
“(2) “Commercial trash compactor” means a structure used by businesses to
collect and compact bulk trash and other waste that is energy efficient and reduces the likelihood
of accidental pollution through spills or wind-blown debris.
“(3) “CTC program” means the commercial trash compactor acquisition grant
program established by section 202.
“(4) “Grant Administration Act” means the Grant Administration Act of 2013,
effective December 24. 2012 (D.C. Law 20-61; D.C. Official Code § 1-328.11 et seq.).
“(5) “Grantee” means a business that meets the criteria and standards established
for the CTC program, as required by section 1094 of the Grant Administration Act and receives a
grant pursuant to this title.
“(6) “Grantor” means the Mayor or the Mayor’s delegate, including a District
agency, board, commission, instrumentality, or other program, or an individual within such an
entity.
“Sec. 202. Commercial trash compactor acquisition grant program.
“(a)(1) There is established a commercial trash compactor acquisition grant program to
financially assist a business or Main Street in the acquisition through purchase or lease of a
commercial trash compactor.
“(2) The CTC program shall be administered by the Mayor pursuant to the Grant
Administration Act; provided, that, notwithstanding section 1095(1) of the Grant Administration
Act, the CTC program grant application process may be established with an open-ended closing,
allowing a business to apply for a grant and for the grantor, subject to subsection (b)(1) of this
section, to approve a grant at any time, in accordance with rules issued pursuant to this title or
the notice established pursuant to section 1094(c) of the Grant Administration Act.

“(b)(1)(A) A grant shall be awarded to eligible applicants in priority order of receipt of
the grant application to the extent that the funds allocated for this purpose in an approved annual
budget and financial plan or available pursuant to subparagraph (B) of this paragraph are
sufficient to allow the grant.
“(B) The Mayor may make available up to $2 million per fiscal year for
the CTC program grants.
“(2) Notwithstanding the provisions of D.C. Official Code § 47-368.06, grants
that may be awarded pursuant to this section may include grants that the Mayor or an agency
receives through an intra-District transfer, a memorandum of understanding, or a reprogramming
from an agency lacking grant-making authority.
“(c) Subject to subsection (b)(1) of this section, the grantor shall award a grantee a grant
in an amount:
“(1) Not to exceed $12,000 toward the leasing of a commercial trash compactor,
the amount to be based on terms and length of time in the lease; or
“(2) Not to exceed $36,000 for the purchase of a commercial trash compactor.
“(d) The Chief Financial Officer may audit the accounts of a business receiving a CTC
program grant up to 3 years following the issuance of the grant.
“Sec. 203. Rules.
“The Mayor, pursuant to Title I of the District of Columbia Administrative Procedure
Act, approved October 21, 1968 (82 Stat. 1204; D.C. Official Code § 2-501 et seq.), may issue
rules to implement the provisions of this title.”.
Sec. 7. Applicability.
(a) Sections 2, 3, 4, and 6 shall apply upon the date of inclusion of their fiscal effect in an
approved budget and financial plan.
(b) The Chief Financial Officer shall certify the date of the inclusion of the fiscal effect in
an approved budget and financial plan, and provide notice to the Budget Director of the Council
of the certification.
(c)(1) The Budget Director shall cause the notice of the certification to be published in
the District of Columbia Register.
(2) The date of publication of the notice of the certification shall not affect the
applicability of the provisions identified in subsection (a) of this section.
