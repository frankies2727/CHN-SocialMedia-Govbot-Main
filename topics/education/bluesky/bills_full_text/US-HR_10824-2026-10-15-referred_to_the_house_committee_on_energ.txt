A BILL
To direct the Secretary of Health and Human Services to establish a standardized stroke education program for emergency medical services clinicians, and for other purposes.
1.Short title; definitions
(a)Short titleThis Act may be cited as the Stroke Education and EMS Care Improvement Act of 2026. (b)DefinitionsIn this Act:
(1)SecretaryThe term Secretary means, unless otherwise specifically provided, the Secretary of Health and Human Services. (2)StateThe term State means one of the 50 States, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the Virgin Islands, Guam, American Samoa, and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands.
2.FindingsCongress finds the following: (1)Stroke is a leading cause of death and disability in the United States, with approximately 795,000 people experiencing a stroke annually.
(2)The outcomes of stroke patients are significantly influenced by the timeliness and appropriateness of pre-hospital care provided by emergency medical services. (3)Variation in emergency medical services (EMS) training and stroke care protocols across different regions of the United States can result in delays in treatment and suboptimal patient outcomes.
(4)Standardized EMS education in stroke care will ensure that EMS professionals are equipped with the knowledge and skills needed to recognize stroke symptoms, perform timely assessments, and route patients to the appropriate stroke care centers. (5)A nationwide standardization of stroke education for EMS clinicians is critical to improving patient outcomes and addressing healthcare disparities in both urban and rural communities.
3.National Standardized EMS Stroke Education Program
(a)Establishment
(1)In generalNot later than 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary shall establish, in accordance with the requirements of this section, a program to be known as the National Standardized EMS Stroke Education Program (in this section referred to as the program). (2)ConsultationIn establishing the program, the Secretary shall consult with—
(A)the Secretary of Transportation; (B)the Federal Interagency Committee on EMS;
(C)the American Stroke Association; (D)the Society for Vascular and Interventional Neurology;
(E)the National Association of Emergency Medical Technicians; (F)National Association of EMS Educators;
(G)the National EMS Advisory Council; and  (H)other relevant stakeholders designated by the Secretary.
(b)Program goalsThe goals of the program shall be— (1)to develop a standardized, evidence-based curriculum that covers the recognition of stroke symptoms, the pre-hospital management of stroke patients, and appropriate transport protocols for different types of strokes;
(2)to ensure national consistency in stroke education across all levels of EMS personnel, including paramedics, emergency medical technicians, and first responders; and (3)to address the specific needs of EMS agencies providing services to underserved and rural areas, ensuring equal access to training and resources.
(c)Program activities
(1)In generalIn carrying out the program, the Secretary shall provide for— (A)the development and implementation of a standardized stroke education curriculum;
(B)the training of EMS clinicians nationwide, including through grants under paragraph (2); and (C)the collection and analysis of data to measure the effectiveness of the program in improving stroke outcomes.
(2)Grants
(A)In generalTo assist with the implementation of the program, the Secretary may make grants, on a competitive basis, to State and local EMS agencies. (B)DistributionIn distributing grants under subparagraph (A), the Secretary shall give priority to State and local EMS agencies that—
(i)provide services to underserved and rural areas; and (ii)face unique challenges in providing timely stroke care.
(3)Stroke care quality improvement measures
(A)Data collection and monitoringThe Secretary shall establish a system to track the effectiveness of the program, including information regarding— (i)the number of EMS clinicians trained in the standardized stroke curriculum;
(ii)the impact of the program on key stroke care outcomes, including the time to treatment, patient mortality rates, and the number of patients transferred to stroke centers; and (iii)State-by-State variations in EMS stroke care and how the program is addressing disparities in access to stroke care.
(B)Annual reportsNot later than 2 years after the date of enactment of this Act, and annually thereafter during the term on the program, the Secretary shall submit to Congress a report that includes— (i)an analysis of the success of the program; and
(ii)recommendations for improvements to the program, including potential changes to the curriculum, delivery methods, or funding structure. (4)Curriculum requirementsThe program shall—
(A)include a comprehensive overview of stroke types; (B)cover recognized stroke scales to assess stroke severity;
(C)address the importance of early recognition, timely intervention, and appropriate routing to certified stroke centers; and (D)provide interactive case studies and real-world scenarios to enhance practical application.
(d)Equity and accessibility considerations
(1)Priority for underserved areasIn distributing funding for training and implementation of the program, the Secretary shall give priority to underserved areas and to those EMS agencies that face barriers to accessing stroke education programs, including— (A)rural EMS systems with limited access to training resources; and
(B)low-income urban areas with high stroke prevalence but limited healthcare access. (2)Technology accessThe Secretary shall ensure that the program is accessible to EMS personnel in remote or underserved areas through online training modules, telemedicine support, and other digital tools.
(e)Authorization of Federal interagency collaboration
(1)Collaboration with other agenciesThe Secretary may collaborate with other Federal agencies, including the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, the Department of Veterans Affairs, and the Federal Emergency Management Agency, to coordinate efforts related to stroke education, training, and healthcare delivery. (2)Cross-agency partnershipsThe Secretary shall work with national EMS organizations and professional medical societies to ensure broad participation and expert input into the development of the program.
(f)Authorization of appropriationsThere are authorized to be appropriated to the Secretary to carry out this section such sums as may be necessary for each of fiscal years 2027 through 2032.
