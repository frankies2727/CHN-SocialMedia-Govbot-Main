RES. 1630IN THE HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVESOctober 19, 2026Mr. Bresnahan (for himself and Mr. Carter of Louisiana) submitted the following resolution; which was referred to the Committee on Education and Workforce, and in addition to the Committee on Energy and Commerce, for a period to be subsequently determined by the Speaker, in each case for consideration of such provisions as fall within the jurisdiction of the committee concernedRESOLUTIONExpressing the importance of raising awareness of opportunity youth and the necessity of investing in and supporting reconnection to education and career pathways across the United States.
Whereas, as of 2024, 10.7 percent of young people ages 16 to 24 in the United States, or 4,336,000 young people, are considered opportunity youth; Whereas opportunity youth are young people who are disconnected from the education and workforce development system and face significant barriers to success, such as homelessness or aging out of the foster care system;
Whereas, according to youth.gov, opportunity youth come from all parts of the United States with rates in suburban communities of 11 percent, urban communities of 13 percent, and rural areas of 19 percent; Whereas the youth labor force participation rate is 9.7 percent lower than its peak in 2000, while employers report nearly 7,300,000 job openings;
Whereas chronic absenteeism, defined as youth missing 10 percent or more of school, reached 28 percent of students in the 2022–2023 school year; Whereas, after 14 years, compared to young people who experienced youth disconnection, those who remained connected earn on average $31,000 more per year, are 45 percent more likely to own a home, are 42 percent more likely to be employed, and are 52 percent more likely to report good or excellent health;
Whereas unemployed youth have significantly worse physical well-being and health outcomes compared to those that are employed; Whereas all young people need to be connected to financial, education, and career pathway resources, supportive mentors and relationships, and opportunities to succeed;
Whereas opportunity youth need these same supports to thrive and succeed; Whereas only around 140,000 of the 4,336,000 opportunity youth across the country are served by the primary federally funded workforce development programs each year;
Whereas workforce development and education programs that are most successful in employment and earnings provide clear, structured career pathways, integrate work and learning, and supportive services; Whereas the Federal Government would gain, on average, an estimated $11,900 per year in additional revenue for each young person who remains connected, or $55,000,000,000 in total; and
Whereas employers benefit from having programs that intentionally hire opportunity youth and report positive experiences in working with opportunity youth: Now, therefore, be it That the House of Representatives—
(1)recognizes the importance of focusing Federal policy on connecting to workforce development, education, and mental health programs, young people (commonly referred to as opportunity youth)— (A)who are disconnected from the education and workforce development system; and
(B)who face significant barriers to success, such as homelessness or aging out of the foster care system;  (2)recognizes the need to robustly invest in, develop, and reauthorize effective Federal programs that support—
(A)the development of opportunity youth; and (B)the connection of opportunity youth to workforce development, education, and mental health programs; and
(3)urges the heads of Federal departments and agencies, including the Secretary of Labor, the Secretary of Education, and the Secretary of Health and Human Services, to take innovative steps to ensure opportunity youth are a priority in Federal workforce development, education, and mental health grant programs.
