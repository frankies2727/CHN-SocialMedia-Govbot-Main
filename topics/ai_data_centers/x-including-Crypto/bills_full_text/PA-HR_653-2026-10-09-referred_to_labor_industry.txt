THE GENERAL ASSEMBLY OF PENNSYLVANIA

HOUSE RESOLUTION
No. 653

HOWARD, OCTOBER 8, 2026
REFERRED TO COMMITTEE ON LABOR AND INDUSTRY, OCTOBER 9, 2026
A RESOLUTION

Directing the Joint State Government Commission to conduct a
study and issue a report on the data that needs to be
collected to evaluate the impact of artificial intelligence
on the workforce.
WHEREAS, The use of artificial intelligence (AI) is growing,

with 54% of job seekers in the United States (US) having an

interview led by AI; and

WHEREAS, Eight in ten hiring managers are looking for
candidates with AI skills; and

WHEREAS, There are growing questions about the bias of AI in

the hiring process, with one study showing that, across three AI

platforms, there was evidence of gender and racial bias; and

WHEREAS, According to employees, 41% of companies in the US
have integrated AI into organizational practices; and
WHEREAS, Half of employees in the US use AI at work, with 13%
of employees using AI daily; and
WHEREAS, It is estimated that 25% of work tasks in the US
could be automated by AI; and
WHEREAS, Yale University's Budget Lab argues that, as of May

2026, there is no evidence of AI disrupting the US labor market;

and

WHEREAS, There is concern about the impact of AI on college
graduates; and

WHEREAS, While unemployment has risen for college graduates

and in professional and business sectors, it is not currently

believed that these jobs were lost due to AI; and

WHEREAS, Some argue that AI will begin to be used by

employers on a large scale within the next 10 years, with the

knowledge and creative sectors potentially seeing the largest

impact; and

WHEREAS, The United States Bureau of Labor Statistics

anticipates that AI will have the largest impact on occupations

that rely on computer usage; and

WHEREAS, Sixty-five percent of employees whose employers

implemented AI in the workplace state that AI has extremely or

somewhat positively impacted their productivity; and

WHEREAS, The percentage of employees with concern over job

displacement due to technology increased to 18% in the first

quarter of 2026; therefore be it

RESOLVED, That the House of Representatives direct the Joint

State Government Commission to conduct a study and issue a

report on the data that needs to be collected to evaluate the

impact of artificial intelligence on the workforce; and be it

further

RESOLVED, That the Joint State Government Commission study:
(1)

The impact that AI may have on this Commonwealth's

workforce and job market.
(2)

The additional data that is needed to evaluate the

impact of AI on this Commonwealth's workforce and job market.

(3)

The impact of job market changes on the General Fund

of the Commonwealth and the cost of social services.
(4)

The data that is being collected by other states and

the Federal Government to evaluate the impact of AI on this

Commonwealth's workforce and job market.

(5)

The data that is being collected by other countries

to evaluate the impact of AI on the workforce and job market;
and be it further
RESOLVED, That the Joint State Government Commission issue a

report with its findings to the Senate and House of

Representatives no later than one year after the adoption of

this resolution; and be it further

RESOLVED, That the Joint State Government Commission submit a
report to all of the following:
(1)

The chairperson and minority chairperson of the

Communications and Technology Committee of the Senate.
(2)

The chairperson and minority chairperson of the

Communications and Technology Committee of the House of

Representatives.

(3)

The chairperson and minority chairperson of the

Labor and Industry Committee of the Senate.
(4)

The chairperson and minority chairperson of the

Labor and Industry Committee of the House of Representatives.
