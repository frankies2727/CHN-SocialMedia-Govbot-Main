AN ACT

Providing for prohibited conduct involving algorithmic pricing
structures and artificial intelligence, for enforcement by
Attorney General and for civil remedies; and imposing
criminal penalties.

The General Assembly of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania

hereby enacts as follows:

Section 1.

Short title.

This act shall be known and may be cited as the Collusive

Algorithms Act.

Section 2.

Findings and declarations.

The General Assembly finds and declares as follows:
(1)

Businesses operating in this Commonwealth

increasingly use artificial intelligence and algorithmic

pricing structures to recommend or set prices and commercial

terms.

(2)

The use of the same artificial intelligence or

algorithmic pricing structure by multiple businesses may

result in artificially high prices that harm consumers in

this Commonwealth, regardless of whether the businesses

knowingly or unknowingly produce the result and whether the

result occurs by design or otherwise.

(3)

Certain artificial intelligence and algorithmic

pricing structures that recommend or set prices are designed

to share nonpublic competitor data and may use the data to

recommend or set artificially high prices in markets

affecting this Commonwealth, producing results similar to

collusion under Federal law.

(4)

Even if not designed to share nonpublic competitor

data, common artificial intelligence may create cybersecurity

risks because a user may unknowingly disclose nonpublic

business data that other artificial intelligence may use to

recommend or set prices in the same industry, including

prices affecting consumers in this Commonwealth, producing

collusion-like results.

Section 3.

Definitions.

The following words and phrases when used in this act shall

have the meanings given to them in this section unless the

context clearly indicates otherwise:

"Algorithmic pricing structure."

A computational process,

including a process derived from machine learning or another

artificial intelligence technique, that processes data to

recommend or set a price or commercial term in this

Commonwealth.

"Artificial intelligence."

A machine-based system that, for

a given set of human-defined objectives, can make predictions,

recommendations or decisions influencing real or virtual

environments through all of the following:

(1)

Perceiving real or virtual environments.

(2)

Abstracting perceptions into models through

automated analysis.

(3)

Using model inference to formulate options for

information or action based on the outcomes under paragraphs

(1) and (2).

"Collusive algorithmic pricing structure."

An algorithmic

pricing structure that uses or incorporates nonpublic competitor

data or was trained using nonpublic competitor data to recommend

or set a price or commercial term in a relevant antitrust

market.

"Common artificial intelligence."

not used exclusively by one person.

"Nonpublic competitor data."

Nonpublic data that is based on

or derived from competitor data of another person that competes

in the relevant antitrust market or is otherwise provided by

that person. The term includes aggregated data but does not

"Nonpublic data."

Information not widely available or

regarding prices, commercial terms and related goods or

Section 4.

(a)

Prohibited algorithmic pricing practices.

Prohibited conduct.--A person may not take any of the

following actions:

(1)

for use in a relevant antitrust market.

Distribute a collusive algorithmic pricing structure

(2)

Develop for distribution a collusive algorithmic

pricing structure for use in a relevant antitrust market.

(3)

Use a collusive algorithmic pricing structure to

recommend a price or commercial term in a relevant antitrust

market.

(4)

Use common artificial intelligence to set a price

for a good or service offered or sold in this Commonwealth.

(b)

Exception.--Subsection (a)(3) does not apply if

substantial evidence establishes that the person did not know

that the collusive algorithmic pricing structure used or

incorporated nonpublic competitor data or was trained using

nonpublic competitor data.

(c)

Criminal penalties.-(1)

A person that violates subsection (a)(1) commits a

felony of the first degree and shall, upon conviction, be

sentenced to pay a fine of not more than $1,000,000 for each

distribution of a collusive algorithmic pricing structure.

(2)

A person that violates subsection (a)(2) commits a

felony of the second degree and shall, upon conviction, be

sentenced to pay a fine of not more than $100,000,000 for

each collusive algorithmic pricing structure developed for

distribution.

(3)

A person that violates subsection (a)(3) commits a

felony of the second degree and shall, upon conviction, be

sentenced to pay a fine of not more than $100,000 for each

pricing decision or recommendation made through use of the

collusive algorithmic pricing structure.

(4)

A person that violates subsection (a)(4) commits a

misdemeanor of the second degree and shall, upon conviction,

be sentenced to pay a fine of not more than $5,000 for each

pricing decision or recommendation made through use of the

common artificial intelligence.

Section 5.

(a)

Enforcement by Attorney General.

Criminal enforcement.--The Attorney General may

investigate and prosecute a violation under section 4.
(b)

Injunctive relief.--If the Attorney General has reason

to believe that a person is engaging in or is about to engage in

conduct prohibited under section 4 and that proceedings would be

in the public interest, the Attorney General may bring an action

in the name of the Commonwealth to restrain the conduct by

temporary or permanent injunction.

Section 6.

Restitution.

Upon issuance of a permanent injunction under section 5(b),

the court may order the defendant, under terms and conditions

established by the court, to restore to a person in interest

money or property, real or personal, acquired by means of a

violation of this act.

Section 7.

(a)

Assurances of voluntary compliance.

Authority.--In administering this act, the Attorney

General may accept an assurance of voluntary compliance from a

person that has engaged or is about to engage in conduct that

the Attorney General considers to violate this act.

(b)

Contents.--An assurance of voluntary compliance may

include a stipulation for a voluntary payment by the alleged

violator, including restitution to consumers of money, property

or other things received from the consumers in connection with a

violation of this act.

(c)

Form and effect.--An assurance of voluntary compliance

shall be in writing and filed with a court of competent

jurisdiction. The assurance shall not constitute an admission of

a violation for any purpose.

(d)

Reopening.--The Attorney General may reopen a matter

closed by an assurance of voluntary compliance at any time for

further proceedings in the public interest under this act.

Section 8.

(a)

Private actions.

Cause of action.--A person that purchases goods or

services and suffers an ascertainable loss of money or property,

real or personal, as a result of a violation under section 4 may

bring a private action in a court of competent jurisdiction

against the person that committed the violation.

(b)

Damages and relief.--In a private action under

subsection (a), a court of competent jurisdiction may award

damages and relief as follows:

(1)

Actual damages arising from the violation or $1,000,

whichever is greater. The court may, in its discretion, award

up to three times the actual damages sustained, but not less

than $1,000.

(2)

Reasonable attorney fees and court costs.

(3)

Additional relief the court deems necessary and

proper.

Section 9.

Regulations.

The Attorney General may promulgate regulations as necessary

to administer and enforce this act.

Section 10.

Construction.

Nothing in this act shall be construed to:
(1)

Limit or preclude an action or remedy available

under Federal antitrust law.
(2)

Except as provided under section 4(a)(4), prohibit

the use of an algorithmic pricing structure or proprietary

artificial intelligence that does not use or incorporate

nonpublic competitor data and was not trained using nonpublic

competitor data.

(3)

Except as provided under section 4(a)(4), prohibit

an algorithmic pricing structure or proprietary artificial

intelligence from using a factor other than nonpublic data to

recommend or set a price or commercial term.

Section 11.

Effective date.

This act shall take effect immediately.
