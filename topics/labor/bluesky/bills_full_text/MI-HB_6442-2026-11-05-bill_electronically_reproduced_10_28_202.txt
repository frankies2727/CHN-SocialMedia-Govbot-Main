Sec. 14s. (1) Notwithstanding any other provision of this act,

the director shall promulgate occupational health standards

regarding employees who are exposed to heat or work in hot

environments that include, but are not limited to, all of the

following:

(a) A requirement for employers to create and implement a plan

to prevent injury or illness related to exposure to heat or work in

hot environments, including, but not limited to, an emergency

response plan.

(b) A requirement for heat monitoring for both indoor and
outdoor work.

(c) Certain requirements that are triggered when temperatures

reach an initial level based on the Wet Bulb Globe Temperature

Index and additional requirements that are triggered when

temperatures reach a level that is hotter than the initial level,

as determined by the director. The requirements under this

subdivision must include, but are not limited to, water and rest

breaks. The duration and frequency of the water and rest breaks

must be correlated to certain temperature levels based on the Wet

Bulb Globe Temperature Index

(d) A requirement for employers to observe employees for signs

and symptoms of injury or illness related to exposure to heat or

work in hot environments.

(e) A requirement for employers to collect and report to the

director data of employee illness, injury and death related to

exposure to heat or work in hot environments. The director shall

publish the data in a manner that the director determines is

appropriate.

(f) Employee training on the prevention of injury or illness
related to exposure to heat or work in hot environments.

(g) A prohibition on employer retaliation against an employee

who reports workplace heat hazards to the department of labor and

economic opportunity.

(h) An exemption for certain employers.

(2) This section does not alter the director's responsibility

to initiate the processing of an administrative rule as required

under section 14(5) if the United States Department of Labor adopts

or promulgates an occupational safety and health standard under the

occupational safety and health act of 1970, 29 USC 651 to 678,

regarding employees who are exposed to heat or work in hot

environments.
