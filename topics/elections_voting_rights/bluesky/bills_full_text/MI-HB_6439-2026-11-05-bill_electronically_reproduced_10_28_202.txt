Sec. 59a. (1) Except as otherwise provided in this subsection,

a covered political advertisement containing a record-based factual

claim must include a clear and conspicuous source disclosure that

directly links to the official public record that supports the

claim. A disclosure that links directly to an official legislative

website maintained by this state is presumed to be compliant with

this section. The secretary of state may approve an alternative to

a source disclosure for a covered political advertisement whose

size or technological format makes compliance with this section

unreasonable.

(2) A covered political advertisement must not state or imply

that a legislator voted for or against a bill unless the

advertisement cites the legislator's final passage vote on the

bill. If a procedural or preliminary action is referenced, the

covered political advertisement must clearly state that the action

was not final passage and disclose the final passage outcome or

state that no final passage vote occurred, as applicable.

(3) A person that receives written notice of an alleged

omission or clerical or formatting error may correct the

advertisement without a civil fine if the person ceases further

dissemination of the noncompliant version and begins a good-faith

correction not later than 48 hours after notice. If notice is

received during the 60 days before an election, the person must

begin a good-faith correction not later than 24 hours after notice.

A cure under this subsection is unavailable for any of the

following:

(a) The mischaracterization of a vote.

(b) The omission of a final passage vote.

(c) A materially deceptive disclosure.

(9) As used in this section:

(a) "Covered political advertisement" means any paid or

sponsored communication disseminated through any medium, including

print, mailers, broadcast television, cable, radio, satellite,

digital or online advertising, video streaming platforms, social

media platforms, websites, mass text messages, mass emails, or

compensated social media influencer content, that is intended to

influence the nomination or election of a candidate or the

qualification, passage, or defeat of a ballot question.

(b) "Final passage vote" means the recorded roll call vote

taken by the full legislative body on the final version of a bill

or resolution that determines whether the measure is passed in the

form before the body. Final passage vote includes a recorded vote

on a conference report or other final legislative action when the

vote determines final passage in that body.

(c) "Knowingly" means having actual knowledge or a reckless

disregard as to whether a disclosure or claim is materially

deceptive.

(d) "Materially deceptive disclosure" means a disclosure or

wrong official, or links to a record that does not address the

claim made in the advertisement.

(e) "Official public record" means a record created, received,

maintained, or published by a federal, state, or local governmental

body during the body's official duties and made publicly accessible

by the body.

(f) "Procedural or preliminary action" means a vote, motion,

amendment, substitute, or committee action occurring before final

passage.

(g) "Record-based factual claim" means a statement that

purports to state a verifiable fact derived from an official public

record, including a claim regarding a final passage vote,

procedural or preliminary action, sponsorship, committee action, or

executive action. A record-based factual claim does not include a

prediction, inference, policy characterization, or statement about

expected effect of legislation or governmental action.

(h) "Social media influencer" means an individual who, in

exchange for compensation or another thing of value, disseminates

political content intended to influence voters on behalf of or at

the direction of a candidate, committee, or political organization.
